    Modules · modules, contract packages, one kernel

    Modules and plug points

    Justin's question: if we rewrite the shell later, does everything else reconnect? The answer drawn: every project is a module that provides one versioned contract package (its plug) and requires other modules only through their contracts. The kernel in the middle is the only place a dependency may live. Click a module for its ports, its swap risk and the three Linear issues that publish, prove and wire its contract.

    The middle tooling: @paperos/kernel

    Fourteen issues in Module System & Swap Tooling. These are the five places a cross-module dependency can exist, so a swap is a search of five places, not the whole repo.

    The swap playbook

    Enforced by paperos module swap <id> --to <impl> (PAP-442), which refuses to advance a step whose gate is red. Rollback at steps 4 to 6 is the flag: flip back in under five seconds, no deploy. Nothing is removed before step 7, so v1 always finds its shapes.

    Chunks · $2,500 of list-price spend = $50 to Justin

    The build in $2,500 chunks, full blast, around the clock

    Sixteen builder sessions run 24/7 over the live blockedBy graph; issues are streamed in finish order and cut into $2,500 chunks of list-price spend, each billed to Justin at $50. Every chunk boundary is a natural stop: nothing in a later chunk is needed to keep an earlier chunk's deliverable working. Switch the model mix to see how the boundaries move.
